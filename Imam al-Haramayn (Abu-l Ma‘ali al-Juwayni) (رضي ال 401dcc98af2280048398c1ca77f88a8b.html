Imam al-Haramayn (Abu-l Ma‘ali al-Juwayni) (رضي الله عنه)

Imam al-Haramayn Abu al-Ma'ali al-Juwaini (d. 478 AH)

Ibn al-Najjar, may Allah have mercy on him, said of him: “The Imam of the jurists in the East and West, and their foremost among both non-Arabs and Arabs—one whose like in virtue the eyes have never seen, and whose eloquence the ears have never heard.”

Al-Mustafad min Dhayl Tarikh Baghdad, Vol. 11, P. 130

Abu al-Ma’ali al-Juwayni, may Allah have mercy on him, said: “If I had known in advance what I have come to realize, I would not have wasted my time on Kalam!”

Siyar A'lam al-Nubala, Vol. 18, P. 473

Abu al-Faraj ibn al-Jawzi, may God have mercy on him, said: “Abu al-Ma’ali al-Juwayni used to say: ‘I have traveled among the people of Islam and explored their knowledge; I have sailed the vast ocean and delved into that which they forbade—all in pursuit of the truth and in flight from blind imitation.

And now I have turned away from all of that to the word of truth.

Adhere to the religion of the elders.

For if the truth does not reach me through the gentleness of His grace, and I die upon the religion of the elders, and my final fate at the time of my departure is sealed with the word of sincerity—then woe to Ibn al-Juwayni!

He used to say to his companions: “O my companions! Do not concern yourselves with Kalam; for if I had known that Kalam would lead me to where they have led me, I would not have occupied myself with them (1)!”

Talbees Iblees, P. 83

Abu al-Fath al-Tabari, the jurist—may Allah have mercy on him—said: (I visited Abu al-Ma’ali during his illness;

and he said: “Bear witness that I have retracted every statement that contradicts the early generations, and that I die upon the same faith as the elderly women of Nishapur!”

Tarikh Al-Islam by Imam Al-Dhahabi, Vol. 10, P. 427

Abu al-Ma’ali al-Juwaini, may Allah have mercy on him, said: “The scholars have differed regarding the apparent meanings found in the Qur’an and Sunnah, and the people of truth have refrained from accepting their literal meanings and applying them according to what the linguists’ understanding suggests from them.

Some of them, therefore, interpreted them, adhering to this approach in the verses of the Qur’an and in what is authentic from the Sunnah of the Messenger, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, and his family.

The scholars of the early generations (Salaf) adopted the approach of refraining from interpretation, applying the apparent meanings to their literal contexts, and leaving their interpretations to the Lord, the Exalted.

What we accept as a view, and by which we affirm the truth of Allah through reason, is to follow the early generations of the Muslim community; thus, it is best to follow them and avoid innovation.
The conclusive textual evidence for this is: that the consensus of the Muslim community is an accepted argument, and it is the foundation of most of the Sharia. The Companions of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah’s peace and blessings be upon him, consistently refrained from delving into its meanings or interpreting what was contained therein, and they were the elite of Islam and the ones who bore the burdens of the Shari’ah. They spared no effort in establishing the principles of the religion, exhorting one another to preserve them, and teaching people what they needed to know from them; For if the interpretation of these verses and apparent meanings were permissible and inevitable, their concern for them would have likely surpassed their concern for the branches of the Sharia; and if their era and that of the Tabi‘un had been marked by a complete abandonment of interpretation, that would have been conclusive proof that this is the accepted approach.”

Al-‘Aqidah al-Nizamiyyah fi al-Arkan al-Islamiyyah” by al-Juwayni (32–33), published by Al-Azhar Library for Heritage, Cairo, Egypt, 1412 AH, edited by Dr. Muhammad Zahid al-Kawthari.

Consider how he distances himself from the Ash‘ari interpretation of the Divine Attributes and returns to the Salaf.
